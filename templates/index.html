      Second screen — a small colour LCD
      
                  A Waveshare 1.69" (240×280) or 1.9" (170×320) LCD can sit next to the e-ink: it shares the
      SPI bus on the other chip-select, so neither screen waits on the other. Both wire the same way.
                  
      
                  
        
        
      
                  
      
                  
        
        
                  
          
                  
            
            
          
                  
          
          
                  
            
            
            
          
                  
          
                  Live, about ten frames a second. It follows the Camera tab's rotation, mirror and fit — the same camera as the e-ink.
                  
        
                  
      
                  
      
                  
        Wiring & pins
        
                  
          
                  VCC → 3.3V, GND → GND, DIN → GPIO10 (MOSI), CLK → GPIO11 (SCLK), then the four below.
          These defaults keep clear of the e-ink HAT (which uses CE0, DC 25, RST 17, BUSY 24 and PWR 18 —
          so the LCD's backlight can't go on 18 the way Waveshare's examples have it).
                  
          
                  
            
            
            
            
            
          
                  
          
                  40 MHz is Waveshare's own figure and fine over short jumpers. If the picture is noisy or the colours wrong,
          try 16 MHz: if that cures it, the wires are the problem; if a fault stays exactly the same at 8 MHz, it isn't the wires.
          The Test card screen shows the glass at its finest.
                  
        
                  
      
                  
      
                  
    
                  
    
                  
      Keys on the HAT
      
                  What each key does. "Prev / Next" turn pages in Read, step coins in Crypto, and fetch a new joke or fortune in Message. Any key wakes the screen when it's off.

        Set up listening
        
                  
          
          
                  
            
            
          
                  
          
          
                  setup.sh installs the listener and its speech model, so this

          Watching the room
          
        
                  
        
                  It compares a small picture every few seconds, which costs almost nothing, and
        only wakes the model when enough has changed.
                  
        
                  
          
          
          
        
                  
        
                  
          
          
        
                  
        
                  
      
                  
      
                  
        
                  
          What it notices
          
        
                  
        
                  Each look is compared with the last, and only changes are kept: Person detected, Package detected,
        Door opened, Cat entered room, Nobody detected. A vision model on your Ollama box sees all of that;
        OpenCV on the Pi alone sees people and cats.

                  Every few minutes it takes in what the camera, the GPS, Home Assistant, the Pi and the other Pis show, thinks it
        over against your goals with the bot, and acts only in the ways you allow below. Each check goes in the journal.
